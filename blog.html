September 12, 2026

What makes an Amazon A+ Content page sell

Category: Amazon

A+ Content sits between your description and your reviews, which is exactly where a reader is deciding whether to buy. Use it to show what the description can only tell: the mood of the book, the series order, and what makes it different from similar titles.

Keep each module simple. One strong image, a short line of copy and a clear takeaway work better than a crowded layout. A comparison chart is useful for a series, since it lets a new reader see where to start and what to read next.

Set up your Goodreads author profile in one afternoon

Category: Goodreads

Start by claiming your author profile and linking every edition of your book to it. Add a clear photo, a short bio written for readers rather than for a publisher, and links to your website and newsletter.

Then tidy each book page. Check that the description matches your Amazon copy, the genres are accurate and books in a series are linked in order. A clean page is easier for readers to shelve, and shelving is what builds your reach.

Your book trailer needs a hook in the first three seconds

Category: Video

Most viewers decide almost at once whether to keep watching. Open with the strongest image or question from your story, not with a logo or a title card. Save the title for the end.

Sound matters as much as pictures. A simple, tense score and one or two carefully chosen sound effects make a trailer feel like a film, even when the visuals are mostly type and motion graphics.

A simple 90-day launch calendar for authors

Category: Launch

Start three months out. In the first month, settle your cover, description, keywords and categories, and begin building your ARC list. In the second, send ARCs, make your trailer and short videos, and set up your Goodreads page.

In the final month, confirm reviewers, schedule social posts and newsletters, and pitch podcasts and blogs. After release day, keep posting and sharing reviews for at least a month while the book is still new.

Build an ARC team that leaves honest reviews

Category: Reviews

Invite readers who already enjoy your genre, and make it clear that you want honest opinions, not only praise. Send the file early, give a review date, and send one friendly reminder.

Keep a simple tracker of who received a copy, who has finished and who has posted. Thank everyone, including those who did not review. A respected team of readers is worth more than a large list of silent ones.
